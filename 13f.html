How to read this without fooling yourself

Form 13F is a delayed, partial photograph of long U.S. exchange-listed equity — not a live portfolio, not shorts, not swaps, not private holdings, not cash.

QuestionWhat 13F can answerWhat it can't
Who owns the most?Largest reported long book vs. largest concentrated $ bet.Beneficial exposure after hedges, shorts, and derivatives.
Who bought the most?Quarter-over-quarter share-count increases and new CUSIPs.Exact trade dates, average price, or a position flipped after quarter-end.
Is this "smart money"?A crowdedness / consensus map.Predictive of next-quarter returns — crowding can be a risk, not an edge.
Why 24 funds, not 9,400?A curated, SEC-verified sample of well-known managers.A claim of statistical representativeness across all institutional money.

Primary source is always the filer's own information table on SEC EDGAR. This page fetches that data directly — nothing here is a hand-typed estimate. CUSIP→ticker mapping is a small hand-maintained lookup (13F filings report CUSIP, never a ticker symbol); unmapped names fall back to the filer's own company name. Filings with more than 600 reported lines (giant multi-strategy books) show summary totals only — full holdings aren't fetched for those, since per-position detail isn't a meaningful signal at that scale.
